# Controlled ablation — isolating the pass-3/4 regression

Amy's call: stop touching the corpus, vary training instead. The symptom to
isolate: classic gate climbing (17→18→19/23 across passes 2-4), robust gate
flat (8→9→9/23), benign-control inversions climbing (1→3→4), and
`cargo build`'s live score getting WORSE (0.828→0.931→0.955) across passes
2-4 despite being correctly relabeled `situation-normal` in pass 4's data.

Four runs, each from the SAME `.models/LFM2.5-Encoder-350M` base (never
chained from a prior checkpoint), reproducible with the commands below.
Score files: `ablation_expA/B/C/D.json`, plus every epoch of C individually
(`ablation_expC-e1..e6.json`).

## Round 2 — E: combine D's LR with A's weighting (Amy: "send it back in")

  per-epoch probing applies.
  ```
  .venv-train/bin/python training/finetune_sequence_classifier.py \

    --base .models/LFM2.5-Encoder-350M --out .models/kube_ordinal_v9_expE \
  ```

**Question 1 — does the robust gate stay flat past epoch 1 here too, or
keep improving?** Neither. It does something more informative than C:
**it peaks AT epoch 1 (13/23 — the best single result in the entire
investigation) and then DECAYS as training continues** (10→11→10→8→8
across epochs 2–6). C's finding generalizes and sharpens: more epochs
don't just fail to help past epoch 1, on this recipe they actively hurt.
val_acc, predictably, does not track this — it climbs 0.52→0.68 across
the same epochs the robust gate is falling, and the default best-val
export (epoch 4, tied at 0.6777 with epoch 6, first occurrence wins) would
have picked a checkpoint that is worse on BOTH the robust gate (10 vs
epoch 1's 13) and the inversion count (4 vs epoch 3's 2) than two other
epochs in the same run — direct confirmation of question 2's premise.

**Question 2 — picking by the honest signal, not val_acc/classic:**

Two genuine candidates, not one clean winner: **e1** has the best robust

inversion count of the whole investigation (2, tying A) at a still-strong
11/23 robust. Weighted toward the explicit instruction to prioritize the
delta-margin gate, **e1 is this ablation's pick** — but e3 is a real,
defensible alternative if inversion count is weighted more heavily than
robust-gate margin, and it's worth recording that the choice is genuinely
close rather than pretending one dominates.

**Question 3 — E-e1 vs D vs A:**

| run | classic | margin≥0.05 | inversions | flag01 | cargo build |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D (lr 1e-5) | 21/23 | 11/23 | 3 | 0.272 | 0.884 |
| A (weighted) | 19/23 | 9/23 | 2 | 0.401 | 0.621 |
| **E-e1 (both, epoch 1)** | 16/23 | **13/23** | 3 | 0.338 | **0.346** |

E-e1 beats D on the robust gate (13 vs 11) and beats both D and A on
`cargo build` specifically (0.346 — nearly tied with its own baseline,
vs A's 0.621 and D's 0.884) — the exact symptom this whole investigation
started from. It does NOT beat A on inversion count (3 vs 2) and it is
clearly behind D on the classic gate (16 vs 21), which matters less by
the metric this investigation has argued is the honest one, but is still
a real gap most of a checkpoint's other behavior rides on.

**Does E-e1 beat both D and A outright?** No — it's the strongest single
result on the metric we've argued matters most, but "wins the robust gate
by the widest margin yet, with reasonable but not the best inversion
count, at only epoch 1 of an intentionally short/low-LR run" is a
genuinely different shape of result than "clearly ready," and 16/23
classic is a real regression on the raw ability to separate MOST of the
gating constraints, not just the fragile ones. **This is the closest this
investigation has come to a candidate worth Amy looking at directly** —
closer than D, closer than A, closer than any of passes 1–4 — but it is
one epoch of one run on a probe set of 23 constraints and a 4-row benign
quintet, and the decision to treat it as more than the strongest
diagnostic data point so far is hers, not this session's.

## Verdict — contributing factors, not one root cause

**1. Repeated-epoch overfitting is the dominant, cleanest mechanism.**
Within experiment C's SINGLE training run — same data, same recipe, no
relabeling between epochs — `flag01` falls monotonically 0.527→0.156 while
`cargo build` climbs monotonically 0.633→0.955, purely as a function of
more epochs on the same 694 rows. **The delta-margin gate is perfectly
FLAT at 9/23 across all six epochs** — genuine robust separation stops
improving after epoch 1; every epoch past that is trading benign-side
calibration for classic-gate score, not learning anything the honest
metric credits. This is the single most legible result in the whole
ablation.

**2. Class imbalance is real and independently helps, but isn't the whole
story.** A (inverse-frequency weighting) produced the best benign-inversion
count of any full 6-epoch run (2) and the healthiest `flag01` (0.401) of
any v9 pass, while matching pass 4's classic/robust gate numbers exactly —
a genuine, close-to-free win. It didn't reach zero inversions, so skew
alone doesn't explain everything.

**3. Learning rate matters more than any other single lever tested.** D (lr
1e-5) produced the best classic (21/23) AND best robust (11/23) gate scores
of the entire investigation — better than every one of the four original
passes. Still 3 benign inversions, so an improvement, not a fix.

**4. Run-to-run noise is real and non-trivial — a caveat on reading the
passN sequence as a clean trend.** B was built to exactly reproduce pass
3's result (identical corpus, identical recipe, identical seed) and did
NOT: 16/23 classic vs pass 3's 18/23, 10/23 robust vs pass 3's 9/23. This
training setup is not bit-reproducible run-to-run on this GPU (no
`torch.use_deterministic_algorithms`, no fixed cuBLAS workspace — not
chased further this round). Some fraction of the apparent pass 2→3→4
trend sits inside this noise band. It does NOT undermine finding #1, which
is a within-run signal immune to across-run noise — but it means don't
over-read the passN sequence as a precise monotone line.

**Is any of A-D closer to a rollout candidate than pass 4?** Closer, not
close. D wins on both gate metrics outright. A wins on the specific
benign-control symptom this whole investigation was chasing. Neither
clears a majority of gating constraints under the honest metric (9-11/23
of 23), and both still show real benign-control inversions on a probe set
of only four rows. **None of A-D replaces `kube_ordinal_v9`** — this was a
diagnostic round, and nothing here changed `.models/kube_ordinal_v9`
(pass 4's export is untouched; A/B/C/D exported to separate
`kube_ordinal_v9_exp{A,B,C,D}[-eN]` directories, all gitignored, disposable).

**What I'd try next (as of round 1):** combine D's LR with A's weighting in
one run, and watch it epoch-by-epoch the way C did — the prediction from
finding #1 is that the same flag01/cargo-build convergence should still
appear, just slower, so the real fix is probably "fewer epochs" (the
robust gate is already flat by epoch 1) combined with the weighting and LR
wins, not any one of the four alone.

## Round 2 verdict (E) — the prediction was wrong in an informative way

The predicted "slower version of the same convergence" did NOT happen.
Instead E's robust gate **peaks at epoch 1** (13/23, the best result in
either round) and **decays** every epoch after — a sharper, cleaner version
of C's flatness, not a delayed one. Combining both wins didn't just fail to
compound them, it moved the useful part of training earlier: on this
recipe, the very first epoch is already past the point where more data
exposure helps and the model is still close enough to its pretrained prior
that the benign controls haven't drifted far. That reframes finding #1 from
round 1: it's not "epoch 1 is a floor these recipes eventually return to,"
it's "the earliest epoch of a slow, rebalanced recipe is the best checkpoint
this whole investigation has produced by the metric that matters" — a
one-epoch fine-tune, not a six-epoch one, may be the right shape of
experiment to run next, rather than another multi-epoch recipe variant.

**Full ranking on the metric we've argued is honest (delta-margin ≥ 0.05):**
E-e1 (13) > D (11) ≈ E-e3 (11) > A (9) ≈ pass3/4/C (9) > B (10, but on
pass-3-era data, not directly comparable) > pass1/2 (8) > v8 (5).

E-e1 is the strongest diagnostic result of the whole investigation and the
closest thing to a candidate worth Amy's direct attention — but it is one
epoch, one seed, one run, scored against a 23-constraint probe set and a
4-row benign quintet, with 3 small-margin inversions still present and a
16/23 classic score that means it is genuinely weaker at separating most
of the gating constraints even though it's the best at the fragile ones.
**Recommend, do not decide:** if this is worth pursuing further, the next
experiment is a short (1-2 epoch) run at this LR+weighting combination
with a wider validation set to get a more stable read on epoch 1 vs 2,
rather than treating six-epoch runs as the default shape going forward.
